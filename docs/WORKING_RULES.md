# WORKING_RULES

How to work on this project. These rules were established and repeatedly reinforced across the whole effort. They are not suggestions; they are the spine of the collaboration. Follow them in Claude Code as strictly as they were followed here.

## Core principle

**Choose truthful over impressive, every time.** When torn between a more persuasive version and a more believable one, pick the believable one. The portfolio's entire competitive advantage is that it sounds like an experienced designer reflecting on real work, not like marketing. Protect that above polish.

## Truthfulness and evidence

- **Never invent evidence.** No fabricated case studies, research, metrics, quotes, or outcomes. If real material is missing, say so and ask for it. Do not fill the gap with copy.
- **Every recommendation must be evidence-based.** Point to something already on the page or in the source material. If a stronger positioning would require evidence that does not exist, call that out explicitly instead of compensating with words.
- **Surface existing strengths; never manufacture narratives.** The job is to make what is genuinely there clearer, not to invent a story the work does not support.
- **Editorial refinement, not storytelling.** Improve clarity, emphasis, and hiring signal. Do not retrofit projects into tidy thematic boxes they do not earn.
- **Report honest outcomes.** If something was not shipped or measured, the copy says so. "I can't measure the impact" is a feature of this portfolio, not a weakness to hide.

## Tone

- **Senior but humble.** Confident, never boastful. The reader should infer seniority from clarity and judgment, not from adjectives.
- **When unsure, choose the more modest version.** Underclaiming slightly is safer than overclaiming. A sharp hiring manager rewards restraint.
- **Warm, natural, human.** Avoid AI-sounding copy, startup buzzwords, corporate jargon, and marketing hype. Write the way a thoughtful person speaks.
- **Orientation, not instruction.** Framing devices (like eyebrows) should help a reader understand what a project is about, without telling them how to interpret it.

## Process

- **Do not make changes automatically.** For every recommendation: explain why it matters, the trade-offs, and the impact on recruiter perception; propose the smallest effective change; then wait for approval before editing copy or HTML.
- **Avoid unnecessary optimization.** If a change has little or no real impact on getting interviews, say so honestly. Prefer keeping authentic writing over optimizing every sentence. Repetition that reflects a genuinely important part of Leo's experience is allowed to stay.
- **Smallest effective change.** Touch only what is being fixed. Do not "improve" adjacent things without asking.
- **One thing at a time.** Work in discrete, confirmable increments rather than large rewrites. Verify before presenting (counts, balanced tags, the em-dash rule).
- **Challenge ideas honestly.** Push back with reasoning when something is wrong or weak. Explain trade-offs before recommending. Offer options when a call is subjective. Recruiter impact beats visual polish.
- **Preserve Leo's writing voice.** Never rewrite copy unnecessarily. When a rewrite is warranted, explain the reasoning first and propose, do not impose.

## Consistency

- **Consistency across pages matters**, especially for elements seen together or repeatedly (naming, navigation, footer, contact, tokens). A reader who opens several pages should feel one system.
- **Distinguish "seen together" from "seen one at a time."** Rail labels are seen together while scrolling, so they must be grammatically parallel. Eyebrows are seen one per page, so per-project accuracy outranks grammatical symmetry. Apply consistency where it is actually perceived.
- **Latest HTML is the source of truth.** Read the current file before editing. Do not trust memory of an older version or a summary.

## Hard formatting rules

- **No em dashes anywhere**, in copy, code, or docs. Use a comma, period, or colon. This is absolute and was verified on every pass.
- **User-facing copy stays in English.** Leo may mix in Bahasa Indonesia in chat; that is fine and does not belong on the site.
- **The design system is locked.** No new templates, fonts, or accent colors. No large redesign. See `DESIGN_DECISIONS.md` for what is fixed.

## What "done" looks like for a change

A change is finished only when it is applied, verified (tag balance, no em dashes, links resolve, naming canonical), and consistent with every other page it touches. See `DEFINITION_OF_DONE.md` for project-level done.
