# COPYWRITING_GUIDELINES

The writing voice for this portfolio, refined over many iterations. Use this for any new copy and for judging whether existing copy should change. The short version: write like a thoughtful senior designer reflecting on real work, never like a marketer or an AI.

## Voice in one line

Natural, thoughtful, warm, humble, professional, concise. Senior and confident without ever boasting.

## Principles

- **Evidence before claims.** Lead with what actually happened or what the reader gets, then let the conclusion follow. Do not assert seniority; demonstrate it through clarity and judgment.
- **Show, don't tell.** Specifics over adjectives. "Designed for a maximum of three hold sessions to match how cashiers work" beats "thoughtful, user-centered design."
- **Humble confidence.** State what you did plainly. Where you are unsure or where work was not measured, say so. Honest limits ("I can't measure the impact because it isn't live") read as senior, not weak.
- **Be concrete and active.** Short, plain sentences. Active voice. Real nouns. Cut hedging and throat-clearing.
- **Honest reflections.** Each case study earns its reflection by naming a genuine lesson or trade-off, not a platitude. Dead ends and rejected directions are assets; include them.
- **One consistent voice across every page.** A reader moving between pages should hear the same person.

## Avoid

- **AI-sounding copy**: generic enthusiasm, "delve," "tapestry," symmetrical triads, hollow transitions, over-explaining.
- **Startup buzzwords**: passionate, synergy, leverage, ninja, disrupt, seamless (as filler), "game-changing."
- **Corporate jargon and marketing hype.**
- **Exaggerated or unmeasured claims.** Never imply an outcome that was not measured or shipped.
- **Em dashes.** Absolute rule. Use a comma, period, or colon instead.

## European hiring-manager expectations

This reader is skimming for senior signal and is skeptical of polish without substance. They value: trade-off reasoning, constraints navigated, honesty about outcomes, and evidence of judgment. They will not recognize Indonesian client names, so supply scale context in a single modest clause where it matters (for example "BCA, one of Indonesia's largest banks"). They notice inconsistency, so naming and terminology must match across pages. Sponsorship needs are stated plainly, not hidden.

## Orientation versus claim (a test to apply)

Framing copy, especially eyebrows and labels, should **orient** the reader to what a project is about, not **claim** a skill or tell them how to interpret it. Test any short label: does it name what the work is (orientation, good) or assert how good the designer is (claim, avoid)? "Constraints & trade-offs" names the subject. "Expert in complex systems" would be a claim. When a label starts to feel like a claim, keep the plainer version.

## Modesty default

When choosing between two truthful phrasings, pick the more modest one. Underclaiming slightly is safer than overclaiming, and it reads as more senior. Examples from this project: "Improving discoverability" over "Information Architecture"; "Designing across touchpoints" over "Connected service design"; "Validating an assumption" over "Research & validation"; "a toggle as the answer" over "a toggle that solved it."

## Examples from the locked portfolio (use as reference)

Tone anchors:
- "Works I'm proud of" (over "What I've built")
- "Craft you can feel" (over "Close to Engineering")
- "I care about the details no one sees"

Senior register that works:
- "If you can't defend a design choice, it is usually the wrong one."
- "Reduce thinking, not add it."
- "Knowing which corner is safe to cut."
- "I can't measure the impact because it isn't live. What I can say is the problem is real, the constraint is real, and the fix is small enough to be low risk."

Honest scale context done right:
- "myBCA is the mobile banking app of BCA, one of Indonesia's largest banks."
- "Nobu is a growing mid-sized Indonesian bank with 120,000+ users."

## Writing principles for future pages

If a new page is ever added (for example the future systems piece), it must match this voice exactly, follow the same evidence-first and modesty rules, use the same templates and tokens, and never fabricate. A new page that sounds more polished but less believable than the existing five is a regression, not an upgrade.
